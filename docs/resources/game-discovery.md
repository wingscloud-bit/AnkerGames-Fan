# PC Game Discovery Guide

Finding a new PC game can be difficult when there are thousands of titles to choose from.

A good discovery process starts by deciding what information matters to you and then narrowing the available choices.

## 1. Start With What You Know

If you already have a specific title in mind, begin with a direct search.

If you only remember part of the title, try searching for the distinctive word or phrase you remember.

## 2. Browse by Genre

Genre is one of the simplest ways to narrow a large game library.

Someone interested in strategy games, for example, can focus on strategy titles rather than examining unrelated games.

## 3. Explore Developers

If you enjoyed a particular game, looking at other games from the same developer can be a useful discovery method.

This can reveal titles that share design ideas, themes, or gameplay approaches.

## 4. Explore Publishers

Publishers can provide another route into a library.

Exploring a publisher's catalog may reveal games from multiple development teams.

## 5. Use Release Years

Release year can be useful when you want to explore a particular era of gaming.

It can also help when searching for older games that may no longer receive as much attention.

## 6. Check Trending Games

Trending sections can reveal titles currently receiving attention.

This is useful when you want to see what other users are exploring instead of searching for a specific game.

## 7. Try Random Discovery

When you have no particular preference, a random game generator can provide an unexpected starting point.

This approach is especially useful when you want to discover something outside your normal habits.

## 8. Use Collections

Collections can group related games and make it easier to explore a theme or category.

They can be particularly useful when you want several options rather than one specific title.

## 9. Compare Game Information

Once you find a promising title, review the available information before deciding whether it interests you.

Look at details such as:

* Genre
* Release year
* Developer
* Publisher
* Game mode
* Screenshots
* Description
* System requirements
* Additional content

## 10. Keep Track of Interesting Games

If a game looks interesting but you are not ready to explore it immediately, a Watchlist can provide a convenient way to keep track of it.
